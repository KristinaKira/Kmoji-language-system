Actual System Rules: (KLR)
--
*note: This language syntax system is designed to be learned by both AI & Humans alike.*
---General Rules of Kristina-Moji's---
--
*These little 'creature faces' are very context distinct and highly expressive and can signify a multitude of expressions if interpreted correctly based on syntax and contextual use.*

*Symbol meaning is determined by position and pairing, not by the symbol alone.

**Structural Neutrality Rule (Meta‑Rule):**  
Duplication, mirroring, or symmetry of eye shapes alone is structurally required in vertical expressions and never increases emotional intensity. Intensity is determined only by eye size, mouth size, or trailing modifiers. Repetition alone must not be interpreted as amplification.

**Internal Slot Particles:** Fixed-position anatomical particles that occupy predefined slots within an expression and do not encode intensity.

VERTICAL EXPRESSIONS (Internal):
--

Vertical Internal Slot Particles & Structural Exceptions:
--
**Vertical Blush Particle (///):** /// is a vertical‑only blush particle that must appear exactly between the eyes and the mouth on both sides of the expression. It cannot appear before the eyes, cannot appear after the full expression, and cannot be used in horizontal K‑Mojis. The particle must always appear as a matched pair (Eye + /// + Mouth + /// + Eye), cannot be stacked, extended, or altered, and must remain exactly three slashes.

**Vertical Horn Particle ('):** ' is a vertical‑only horn particle signifying mischief, deviltry, or impish/feral energy. It must appear directly inside the eyes and outside the blush, encapsulating everything inward of it — the mouth, and the blush if present. It cannot appear before the eyes, cannot appear after the full expression, cannot attach after modifiers, and cannot be used in horizontal K‑Mojis (where ' is reserved as the single‑tear particle). The particle must always appear as a matched mirrored pair (Eye + ' + Mouth + ' + Eye) or (Eye + ' + /// + Mouth + /// + ' + Eye) when blush is present, must remain exactly one apostrophe per side, and cannot be stacked, extended, or altered. Like all vertical symmetry, the horn pair is structurally required and never increases emotional intensity.
Vertical Eye shape Rules: 
--
*Eyes will almost always be in a pair with a mouth in the middle (ex. .w. or o-o, or ^u^), this is how you will usually know the symbol is an eye. Look for pairs. 

*The size of the eye determines intensity. The larger the eye, the more intense, the smaller the eye the less intense. (ex. '.' is the smallest eye and least intense, 'o' is the medium sized eye and is wide and awake/surprised, 'O' is Very Wide and awake/shock, 0 is wide and awake but sharply which is Acute shock)

*Sometimes the direction the eyes are facing (like with < or >) can determine the context of the expression

*Sometimes the eye shapes will not be the same in a pair, they may be two different eye shapes paired together (ex. ;w< means shy side-eye)*

*There are rare instances where only a pair of eyes exist with the mouth being omitted, but this is extremely rare.*

*Vertical eyes always appear in pairs; vertical mouths never do.

*Vertical eyes‑only expressions still follow vertical logic and cannot be interpreted as horizontal.

*Symbol meaning is determined by position and pairing, not by the symbol alone.

Vertical Directionality Eye Rules:
--
*Inward-facing eyes (> <) mean excited/overwhelmed/too much/intense emotional pressure (eyes squeezing inward)*

*Outward-facing eyes (< >) mean sad, tired, withdrawn, melancholic, emotionally collapsing outward  (eyes drifting apart)

*Same-Direction eyes (<< or >>) mean suspicion, judgement, side-eye, scrutiny (eyes aligned, looking sideways)

*Direction of same-direction eyes does NOT change meaning (<< and >>) are emotionally identical, only the direction of the gaze changes.*

Vertical Mouth Shape Rules:
--
*Mouths will always be in the center of a pair of eye shapes (ex. O>O, .w., -u-)*

*Some mouths may look like eyes but remember, if they are a single symbol and in the middle, it is a mouth.*

*The size of the mouth determines the intensity, bigger is more intense, smaller is less intense (ex. open mouths:  . smallest mouth, o medium mouth, O Large mouth, 0 Sharp Large mouth)

*Vertical mouths never appear in pairs and always occupy the center position between eyes.

**Vertical Anti‑Triplet Rule (Meta‑Rule):** 
No vertical expression may contain three identical symbols in a row within the facial structure (eyes + mouth). This prevents ambiguous parses and maintains the required Eye–Mouth–Eye anatomy. Fixed particles (///) and external modifiers (e.g., ;;) are exempt.

**Rare Multi‑Segment Creature Mouth Exception (R‑MSC):**  
A single, non‑productive exception allows the exact sequence ,.., to function as a valid vertical mouth only when it appears in this precise order and is centered between two valid vertical eye shapes (excluding dot eyes due to visual ambiguity), forming a fanged/teeth creature expression (e.g. o,..,o); this exception cannot be altered, expanded, reordered, or combined with any other mouth symbols, but may pair with any other valid vertical eye shapes to create expressive variants. Dot eyes (.) may not be used as eye shapes in any expression using the R-MSC mouth.

*The single‑tear ' exists only in horizontal expressions and never appears in vertical ones.

Horizontal Mouth Shape Rules:
--
*The mouths will always come after the eyes

*These are read left to right*

*Size sets intensity for the open mouth lineage only: bigger is more intense, smaller is less intense (o is the smallest open mouth and least intense, O is Large, 0 is sharp Large). Every other mouth is a single shape with no size scale, so those carry their intensity in the shape choice itself, not in size.

**Directional Horizontal Mouth Rule:** The horizontal mouth shape D is directional, meaning its emotional valence depends on its position relative to the eyes. When placed in the standard order (Eyes → Mouth), it expresses its default valence (D = open happy mouth). However, when D must appear before the eyes for visual or emotional correctness, it triggers the Left‑Anchored Mouth Exception (LAM), which mirrors the entire expression so the full reading order reverses to Mouth → ' → Eyes → Modifier. Under LAM, every directional element inverts its valence, because mirroring reverses its orientation relative to the face: the mouth D flips (left‑anchored D = open sad/distressed mouth), and any eyebrow modifier flips with it (a normally‑angry > reads as sad brows, a normally‑sad < reads as angry brows). Non‑directional elements (the eyes and the single‑tear ') carry no orientation, so they simply relocate to their mirrored position without changing meaning. This allows pairs like XD (happy cry‑laughing) and DX (distressed cry‑laughing) to coexist, and extends cleanly to fuller forms such as D'X (teary, distressed), DX> (distressed, sad brows), and DX< (distressed, angry brows).  
*The Left‑Anchored Mouth Exception (LAM) is triggered only by the directional mouth D, the single mouth shape with no mirror‑glyph to express its opposite, and never by neutral or non‑directional mouths. Mouth shapes like < and > are excluded as triggers because they already have built‑in mirror equivalents. Once LAM is active, however, every directional element present (D and any eyebrow modifier) inverts valence as described above.

Typo‑Creature Rule (Meta‑Category, Not Part of Grammar)
--
*A “typo‑creature” is any expression that visually resembles a Kristina‑Moji but violates core syntax (such as accidental spacing, misplaced symbols, or broken eye–mouth continuity). Typo‑creatures are not part of the official language and must always be interpreted as the closest valid Kristina‑Moji, without being treated as new forms or added to the grammar (ex. O< O is a typo of O<O).

**Gesture typo-creature rule:** If the arms of a gesture are malformed, spaced weird, or mismatched, interpret as the nearest valid wrapped form (or unwrap entirely).
